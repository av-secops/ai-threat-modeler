NEXORA TELECOM BSS - PRODUCTION RELEASE 26.09
Document revision: architecture-1

This is a fictional telecom business-support-system assessment scenario. It is
inspired by the product category of platforms such as Amdocs Optima, not by any
claim about Amdocs' actual architecture, controls or vulnerabilities.

1. BUSINESS CONTEXT

Nexora serves 25 telecom operators and MVNOs across multiple countries. It manages
approximately 12 million subscriber accounts and processes approximately 400
million usage records daily. These are scenario assumptions, not vendor claims.
The platform supports prepaid and postpaid mobile, broadband, enterprise plans,
bundles, roaming settlement, partner sales and customer self-service.

Each operator is a tenant. A tenant owns subscriber identities, tariffs, usage,
wallets, invoices, service orders, support cases and partner settlement records.
Enterprise customers can contain multiple billing accounts. Operator support
agents must not access another operator's records. Platform support has a separate
emergency-access workflow. Resellers receive narrower, delegated permissions.

Security objectives include preventing cross-tenant access, fraudulent balance
changes, incorrect charges, duplicate provisioning, unauthorized plan changes,
payment replay, disclosure of subscriber information, and interruption of billing.

In scope: the following 16 independently deployed microservices, their AWS
infrastructure, delivery identities, customer and support applications, partner
interfaces and payment integrations. Operator radio/core-network internals,
physical SIM security, endpoint malware and payment-provider internals are outside
this model. Their interfaces remain in scope. No AI or LLM subsystem is deployed.

2. DEPLOYMENT CONTEXT

Model environment: production.

Primary region: eu-west-1. Recovery region: eu-central-1.
Production application account: 111122223333.
Security logging account: 444455556666.
Build and artifact account: 777788889999.
Account identifiers are fictional examples and contain no credentials.

All 16 microservices C1-C16 run as separate deployments on Amazon EKS, using
managed EC2 node groups in private subnets across three availability zones.
The EKS control-plane endpoint is private. Each service has its own Kubernetes
service account and IAM role for service accounts. The actual permissions vary;
do not assume that separate identities imply least-privilege policies.
GitHub Actions builds images, ECR stores images, and Argo CD deploys releases.
EKS is a hosting platform, not an extra application hop between every service.

Aurora is a shared PostgreSQL cluster with separate service schemas and database
roles. This is shared infrastructure, not a separate database cluster per tenant.
DynamoDB stores usage deduplication and charging state. Redis stores sessions and
caches. MSK carries usage and business events. SQS carries provisioning jobs.
The invoice/document bucket and public product-assets bucket are separate buckets.
KMS, Secrets Manager, CloudWatch and CloudTrail are infrastructure services.
Do not count AWS managed services or external providers as extra microservices.

6. PRIMARY BUSINESS WORKFLOWS

W1 - New subscriber: Customer Portal -> Public Edge -> Application Ingress ->
Order Orchestration Service. Order Orchestration Service separately calls Customer
Account Service, Subscriber Profile Service and Offer Pricing Service, then sends
a command to Provisioning Queue. Service Provisioning Service consumes the command,
calls Operator OSS and publishes a completion event to Event Stream. Order
Orchestration Service consumes that event. The three lookup services are not
serial hops and do not publish the provisioning command.

W2 - Prepaid usage charging: Usage Collectors -> Public Edge -> Application
Ingress -> Usage Mediation Service -> Event Stream -> Rating Charging Service.
Rating Charging Service calls Offer Pricing Service for the tariff, then separately
calls Balance Wallet Service for a debit. Balance Wallet Service accesses Business
Database. Offer Pricing Service does not call Balance Wallet Service.

W3 - Postpaid billing: rated-charge events -> Invoice Billing Service -> Business
Database and Billing Documents -> invoice-ready event -> Notification Service.

W4 - Payment and top-up: Customer Portal -> Payment Collections Service through
the edge and ingress -> Payment Provider. Customer Portal separately opens the
provider-hosted checkout for card entry. The provider sends a separate callback
through the edge and ingress to Payment Collections Service, which may then call
Balance Wallet Service. A browser redirect is not payment-settlement evidence.

W5 - Operator support/export: Operator Portal -> Workforce Identity for login;
authenticated requests traverse the edge and ingress to Customer Support Service
or Document Export Service. These services must enforce operator-tenant scope.

W6 - Roaming settlement: rated usage events -> Partner Settlement Service ->
Business Database and Billing Documents -> Roaming Partner.
Partner Settlement Service accesses those stores and separately sends statements
to Roaming Partner; the stores do not call the partner. Operator Portal submits
settlement adjustments and approval requests through the edge and ingress to
Partner Settlement Service.

7. KNOWN ISSUES IN PRODUCTION RELEASE 26.09

The following are explicit owner-declared weaknesses in this fictional scenario.
They are not penetration-test results and must not be called runtime-verified.

K01. Subscriber Profile Service accepts X-Tenant-ID from the request and uses it
for database filtering without verifying that the authenticated subject belongs
to that tenant. JWT signature validation still occurs.

K02. Offer Pricing Service concatenates the sort/filter expression supplied to
its administrative offer-search endpoint into a PostgreSQL query. Its ordinary
price-quotation endpoint uses parameterized queries.

K03. Order Orchestration Service trusts tenant_id and requested_action inside
provisioning-completion events. It does not bind the event producer identity to
the tenant or validate that the referenced order permits the requested transition.

K04. Service Provisioning Service accepts an operator callback URL configured by
a reseller and makes an outbound request without destination allowlisting or
checking resolved addresses against private and link-local destinations.

K05. Usage Mediation Service rejects duplicate record IDs only while a Redis
cache entry exists for 15 minutes. The persisted DynamoDB checkpoint does not
enforce uniqueness for the full billing replay window. Replayed usage records
can therefore be charged again after the cache expires.

K06. Rating Charging Service has no workload-specific concurrency quotas or
per-tenant worker isolation. A single operator's bulk replay can occupy the worker
pool shared with interactive prepaid charging. Public edge throttling is separate.

K07. Balance Wallet Service implements debit as a read, balance check and later
write without an atomic conditional update or equivalent serialized transaction.
Concurrent debit requests can pass the check against the same old balance.

K08. Invoice Billing Service issues invoice download URLs valid for 24 hours.
Those URLs remain usable after customer account suspension or invoice access
revocation; possession of a previously issued URL is sufficient for the download.

K09. Payment Collections Service has a deprecated production route,
/payments/webhooks/legacy, that skips webhook signature validation and does not
reject previously processed provider event IDs. The primary webhook route has
different controls, described in the supporting document.

K10. Partner Settlement Service permits the same operator employee to modify a
settlement adjustment and approve the resulting payout batch without independent
approval or an enforced separation-of-duties check.

K11. Customer Support Service caches support entitlements for 12 hours. Password
reset, workforce account disablement and removal of a privileged support role do
not invalidate existing sessions or cached entitlements immediately.

K12. Document Export Service writes customer-supplied fields into CSV output
without neutralizing spreadsheet formula prefixes. Exported fields can include
subscriber names and customer-entered company labels.

K13. Document Export Service has an IAM role allowing s3:GetObject and
s3:ListBucket over every tenant prefix in Billing Documents, even though each
export job is intended to operate for one tenant. A worker compromise crosses
the intended object-access boundary unless another control prevents that path.

K14. Event Stream uses a shared Kafka application principal for Rating Charging
Service, Invoice Billing Service and Partner Settlement Service. Its topic ACLs
allow each of those producers to write to order-completion and payment topics
that their business role does not require.

K15. Deployment Controller deploys some production services using mutable image
tags rather than immutable digests. Container signature enforcement is planned
for release 26.10 and is not enforced in 26.09.

K16. Customer Support Service's troubleshooting mode writes bearer tokens and
full subscriber identifiers into Application Logging. The support workload IAM
role can also delete its own CloudWatch log group. Security Audit has a separate
cross-account CloudTrail destination and must not be assumed deletable by that role.

8. CONTROL AND EVIDENCE INTERPRETATION

Upload the accompanying supporting-controls.md document for the implemented
controls. Silence in this architecture prompt is not evidence that a control is
absent. Explicit exceptions and narrower scopes must remain visible.
Neither WAF presence, encryption at rest nor valid JWT signatures proves tenant
authorization, correct payment state transitions, query safety or safe exports.
Assess remaining unknown controls as questions or potential risks, not as confirmed
missing implementations. Keep distinct root causes separate and merge genuinely
duplicate findings only when the affected control and scope are the same.

The requested review is a complete STRIDE assessment of this declared architecture,
including component threats, trust-boundary flows, identity and IAM, async business
logic, source/build integrity, payment processing and telecom charging integrity.
Do not add technology-specific threats for technologies absent from the inventory.
